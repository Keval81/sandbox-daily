# You Can't Un-Elect a Man With a Grudge and a Stamp

You cannot overthrow a king by asking for your birthday card back.

Yet that, more or less, is the plan. Europe's football associations spent the past year writing warm letters endorsing Gianni Infantino for a fourth term as president of Fifa. Now Uefa has declared it no longer has confidence in him, and its members are weighing whether to take those letters back before nominations close in November. It is the political equivalent of un-friending someone and hoping they notice.

## The Setup

Fifa has 211 member associations, and by July 2026 more than 200 of them had sent letters backing Infantino. His re-election looked like a done deal — what one might call a fait accompli, which is French for "don't bother turning up." Of Uefa's 55 members, 52 wrote endorsement letters. Only three held back: Germany, Romania and Norway.

Then came a scheme Infantino privately backed, called Fifa Forward Enterprise. In essence it was a plan to sell off chunks of the World Cup — the tournament, sliced up and sold like a house being cleared before the owners emigrate. Uefa's members threatened to boycott future Fifa competitions, and the plan was largely scuppered.

That was the spark. The mood across Europe is now febrile, with a widespread appetite to remove Infantino quickly. Two methods are on the table: a mass, simultaneous withdrawal of the letters, or associations peeling away one by one. Either way, a country must formally notify Fifa that it is recalling its endorsement. The whole thing would, in effect, amount to a vote of no confidence.

## You Can't Beat Somebody With Nobody

And it would achieve precisely nothing, because Europe has forgotten the oldest rule in politics: **you cannot beat somebody with nobody.**

No successor has emerged. Not one name. The rebels have a villain, a grievance and a stack of letters they'd like returned, but they do not have a candidate. Efforts to find one are being "stepped up," which is the sound a group makes when it has agreed on the enemy but not the alternative. And until that person exists — and exists before the 18 November nomination deadline — the withdrawal of letters is pure theatre, a standing ovation for an empty stage.

Worse, a European candidate on their own is useless. Uefa's 55 associations sound impressive until you remember they are roughly a quarter of the electorate. A quarter wins you nothing. Infantino, meanwhile, can still expect Africa's CAF and South America's Conmebol to lean his way — neither confederation joined Europe's revolt over the sell-off, with Africa staying neutral and Conmebol merely promising to "evaluate" the plan. Do that maths and the revolt starts to look less like a coup and more like a strongly worded group email.

## The Breakdown

Let me explain why the letters barely matter, using the only model that fits: a school election.

Imagine a class of 211 students voting for class president. Every student gets one vote. Before the vote, most of them signed a card saying "we like Gianni." Now a bloc of 52 European students wants to cross their names off the card.

Crossing your name off a card is not a vote. The actual vote is scheduled for March 2027 — but there is an earlier deadline that matters far more. Any challenger must formally enter the race by 18 November 2026, four months before the ballot. That deadline is the single most important fact in this entire story, and it belongs to Infantino, not his enemies. Europe does not have seven months to find someone. It has roughly three and a half.

Because here is how the voting hall splits. The African delegates (CAF) lean to Gianni. The South American delegates (Conmebol) lean to Gianni. The European delegates (Uefa) are furious, but they're a quarter of the room and they're shouting at a wall. That leaves two blocs who decide everything: Asia (the AFC) and North and Central America (Concacaf). These are the crucial battlegrounds, and any challenger would need real support from both to stand a chance.

Now watch what Infantino does with his time.

Qatar recently declared for him in public — one more Asian endorsement banked, from the very region the rebels need to win. And Qatar is not a one-off; the endorsements keep arriving from across the continent, one federation at a time. It's the political version of turning up to a job interview and slapping down references faster than anyone can read them.

This is the whole game. Infantino is determined to stay on and is lobbying members across the globe. His method isn't clever. It's just relentless. While Europe holds meetings about the correct etiquette for un-endorsing a man, he collects Asian federations one handshake at a time and lets the calendar do the rest. Run out the clock to 18 November, keep the endorsements flowing, and if no rival has filed by then the March 2027 vote arrives with a single name on the ballot.

And the AFC is the perfect hunting ground for this, because the AFC rarely votes en masse. It does not move as a block. That cuts both ways: some Asian associations unhappy with Infantino could rally to the European cause, but a divided confederation is far easier to pick off vote by vote than to flip all at once. A man collecting individual endorsements loves a group that won't act together.

There is one delicious wrinkle. European sources are quietly wondering whether the federations lining up behind him actually know how much dissent Infantino is facing — the suggestion being that they've just publicly backed a man whose ship may be taking on water. It's a lovely bit of spin, but it also gives the game away. If your best argument is "our opponents don't realise they're supporting the wrong person yet," you are not winning. You are hoping.

We should also remember how these letters were gathered in the first place. Back in July, Fifa applied heavy pressure on wavering members even when Infantino's position looked perfectly safe. Many signed while privately grumbling about him. So a good chunk of that "more than 200" was never devotion — it was people signing to avoid a scene. Those signatures peel off easily. But a signature that peels off easily is also a signature that means very little, which brings us right back to the problem: subtraction is not the same as victory.

## The Takeaway

Strip away the drama and the position is simple. Europe can recall every letter it ever wrote, issue its vote of no confidence, and stage the most coordinated tantrum in the history of the sport. Infantino will still be Fifa president, because none of it is a vote — and unless Europe fields a challenger by 18 November, the March ballot may carry no alternative to vote for at all.

The rebels are fighting the wrong war. They think the story is the letters. The story is the search — the frantic, so-far-fruitless hunt for one human being who can carry Europe and also win in Asia and the Americas. Find that person before 18 November, and the withdrawals become a genuine threat. Fail to find them, and Europe will have spent 2026 loudly un-signing cards while Infantino quietly signs up the rest of the planet.

Nobody has ever been removed from office by a coalition whose flagship policy is asking for the stationery back. Until Europe names a name, Gianni Infantino isn't fighting a rebellion. He's watching one rehearse.
