# Putin Threw a Party for 130 Countries. Ukraine Sent 140 Drones.

There is a particular kind of comedy in spending months telling the world your country is an "island of stability," then having to order the residents of your second city to stay indoors while you're still on stage. On 6 June 2026, on the final day of Russia's flagship economic forum, that is roughly what happened in St Petersburg.

## What Actually Happened

Russian authorities called it "unprecedented." More than 140 drones were shot down over the Leningrad region. Aleksandr Drozdenko, the region's governor, reported a fire at a military facility he declined to name, along with evacuations and what he described as "insignificant" damage to buildings. Alexander Beglov, the governor of St Petersburg, told residents to stay inside — the first such order since the war began more than four years ago.

President Zelensky said Ukrainian forces had hit "the enemy navy's arsenals and a base in Kronstadt," home to the main outpost of Russia's Baltic Fleet. He called it a "just response" to Russian attacks and added that it was "time to end this war." The target sat 1,000 kilometres — 620 miles — from Ukrainian-held territory.

The timing was not an accident. The forum had drawn thousands of guests from 130 countries, the whole point of which is to convince foreigners to bring their money. A day earlier, drones had already hit the city's outskirts as the event opened.

## The Strike Was Aimed at the Wallet, Not the Front Line

Here's the thing most coverage will miss while it counts drones and measures distances: this attack had almost nothing to do with the battlefield and almost everything to do with a sales pitch.

You fly in thousands of people from 130 countries, you put out the good chairs, and you spend the week whispering that Russia is calm, functional, and open for business — a safe harbour while the rest of the world wobbles. The entire production depends on one unspoken promise: *nothing bad happens here.*

So when air defences are lighting up the sky over your investor conference and the mayor is telling everyone to hide, the product you're selling has a rather visible defect. You cannot run a stability expo during an air raid. The strike didn't need to destroy the Baltic Fleet. It only needed to be **inconvenient at exactly the right moment** — and on that score it was a triumph.

The detail that gives the game away is the US delegation. It showed up low-key, the first American presence in many years. Putin wanted that photo. Ukraine made sure the backdrop included evacuations.

## How a Country Starts Flying 1,000 Kilometres Into Enemy Territory

Let's make this concrete, because the numbers are easy to skim past and they shouldn't be.

Imagine your neighbour keeps throwing rocks over the fence. For years you could only throw rocks back across the same fence. Then one day you build a slingshot that reaches not just your neighbour's garden, but the office tower downtown where he meets his bankers. That's the shift. St Petersburg is 1,000 km away. A Krasnodar oil depot, also struck, was 500 km out.

And the volume tells you this is industrial, not improvised. Since early May, more than 200 lorries and more than 30 fuel trucks have been hit. That's not a lucky shot — that's a production line meeting a target list. Ukraine has kept its focus on energy and oil, the stuff that funds the whole war. Zelensky has taken to calling these strikes "long-range sanctions," which is a wonderfully dry euphemism for blowing things up. The difference is that ordinary sanctions are signed in a conference room and ignored. These ones arrive personally.

Then there's the line from one Ukrainian unit commander, speaking to the BBC, that should genuinely worry Moscow: *"We fly in Russia like it's our own territory. Almost no resistance, not hard to reach a target."* Read that again. That is not the language of a desperate underdog lobbing the occasional firework. That's the bored confidence of a courier who knows the route. When your enemy describes your homeland the way a delivery driver describes his patch, your air defence has a branding problem.

The knock-on effects show how deep the rot goes. In occupied Luhansk, the Moscow-installed authorities suspended coach services on two motorways, banned commuter trains, and halted the group transport of children — all blamed openly on Ukrainian drone strikes on logistics. When you're cancelling the school bus and admitting why, the "island of stability" slogan has officially capsized.

## The Negotiation Nobody Wants to Have

The political backdrop makes the strike sharper still. The day before the drones arrived, Putin used the forum to announce there was no point meeting Zelensky. A truce, he said, would only let Ukraine regroup. He'd end the war when Russia's goals were met — and those goals remain enormous: Ukraine surrenders Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia, and abandons any hope of NATO.

Zelensky, for his part, had sent an open letter calling for a ceasefire and face-to-face talks. Ukraine refuses to hand over any territory, on the entirely reasonable logic that rewarding an invasion is the best way to guarantee the next one. He warned it would be "wrong to simply wait."

So one man says *no point talking,* the other says *don't just sit there* — and into that deadlock flies a swarm of drones with impeccable comic timing. If you say there's no point negotiating because your enemy is too weak to matter, it's awkward to make that argument while 140 drones are being shot down over your investor gala.

Meanwhile Trump, who has been mediating, has been pulled away by the war with Iran, which has recently taken precedence. The referee, in other words, has wandered off to a different fight, leaving the two sides to talk past each other through governors' press statements and aerial demolition.

## The Takeaway

Forget the drone count and the damage assessments, which Russia will minimise and Ukraine will inflate, as both always do. The real message of 6 June was delivered to the audience, not the target.

For four years Russia's pitch to the watching world has been that the war is a faraway thing, safely quarantined near the border, and that everything back home runs on time. Ukraine has now demonstrated, on the most public day of the most public event Russia stages, that it can reach 1,000 kilometres into the heart of that story and switch off the lights. You cannot sell stability while your guests are being told to take cover.

That's the thing about an island of stability. It only works as a slogan until someone proves they can swim to it whenever they like.
