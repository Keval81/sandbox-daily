# The Man Who Died in Season Two and Never Left

Here is a trick almost no actor manages: appear in all six seasons of a show while getting murdered in the second one. Vincent Pastore pulled it off.

Pastore has died aged 80. He was found at his home in the Bronx — the same borough where he was born in 1946, which is a tidy kind of symmetry if you like your life stories to end where they start. CBS News, the BBC's US partner, reported no indication of criminality. No cause of death was given. Robert Attermann, his manager for more than thirty years, confirmed it.

He came to the work late. He enlisted in the US Navy during the Vietnam War, then took up acting afterwards — a career path that skips the drama-school small talk entirely. There was *Goodfellas*, *Carlito's Way*, then *Law & Order*, *General Hospital*, *Hawaii Five-O*. Solid, steady, the résumé of a man who kept getting hired.

But you know him as one person: Salvatore "Big Pussy" Bonpensiero.

## Known For A Haunting, Not A Role

The obituaries will reach for the lazy phrase — "best known for one role" — and file him away. They will be wrong. Pastore isn't known for a role. He's known for a haunting.

Think about how television normally treats a dead character. They get killed, the plot moves on, and by the next season nobody mentions them, as if the writers signed a legal agreement to pretend they never existed. Death is usually the exit door: you leave through it and you don't come back.

Big Pussy refused to use the door.

## The Best Friend Problem

Here is the setup, in case you skipped the golden age of prestige TV. Big Pussy was Tony Soprano's best friend. He was also, it turned out, an FBI informant — a rat wearing the face of a brother. The BBC called his death in series two "one of the show's most memorable storylines," which is the polite way of describing the moment his best friends executed him on a boat.

That scene is the whole point. Most murders on television are about the victim. This one was about the men holding the gun. The show killed him off early precisely so it could keep him around — a permanent bruise the cast kept pressing whenever they needed to remember what betrayal costs.

Do the arithmetic. Killed in season two, present in all six. That means four full seasons of an actor turning up *after* his character had been shot and dumped. Guilt, memory, the odd hallucination — Pastore worked more once dead than half of Hollywood manages alive.

## The Award He Won As A Team Sport

He wasn't a leading man and never pretended to be. In 2000 he won a Screen Actors Guild award, but the specific one — Outstanding Performance by an Ensemble in a Drama Series — is shared with the whole cast. It is the trophy you win for making everyone around you better rather than for hogging the light. That fits.

Attermann said the quiet part out loud. "To the world, he will always be remembered as the unforgettable 'Big Pussy,' but to those of us who knew him, he was so much more." He described a man "passionate about his craft," and, in the detail that lands hardest, "always encouraging, respectful, and generous with young actors." The man who spent HBO's best years playing the ultimate betrayer apparently spent his real life doing the opposite.

There is something almost funny in that gap. The face of the informant, off camera, was the one taking the newcomers aside and telling them how to hit their mark.

Most actors chase immortality and settle for a decent showreel. Vincent Pastore got the rarer thing. He played a man his friends could not bury, and the show never once let him rest — which is exactly how we should remember the actor who made him.
